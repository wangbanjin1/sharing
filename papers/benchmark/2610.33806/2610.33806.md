Abstract

Language-model agents must adapt as users

revisetheirrequirementsandprioritiesduring

complex tasks. We introduce IntentDelta, a

human-constructedmulti-turnbenchmarkwith

638 trajectories and 3,766 turns across Web-

Shop and TravelPlanner. Each turn pairs a

userutterancewithacumulativeintentionstate

andhumanreferenceactions. Thetrajectories

are cross-verified and cover feasibility- and

preference-driven changes in constraints and

priorities. We evaluate eleven agents on in-

tention recovery and action quality. Average

IntentionRecoveryis33.1%inTravelPlanner

and 44.7% in WebShop, while average Ac-

tion Score is 31.6% and 41.0%, respectively.

Agents struggle to preserve earlier require-

ments and distinguish soft preferences from

must-have constraints. Correct intention re-

coveryalsodoesnotguaranteesuccessfulex-

ecution,highlightingchallengesintranslating

evolvinguserintentionsintoactions.

Successful task execution requires both valid ac-
